# AI QUANT LAB — Production Readiness Checklist v1.0

**Document Class:** Constitutional Readiness Checklist  
**Authority:** AI Quant Lab Deployment and Operational Governance  
**Status:** v1.0  
**Applies To:** Every strategy, portfolio, implementation, deployment candidate, monitoring configuration, and production-like operational scope

## 1. Production Readiness Philosophy

Production readiness is the evidenced ability to operate an exact governed scope under exact authority, versions, controls, owners, monitoring, failure handling, and reconstruction. It is not a prediction of success or a substitute for prior gates. Readiness exists separately for paper, limited live, live active, scaling, and monitored production; no state inherits the next state's authority.

## 2. Production Readiness Principles

No readiness without identity, exact scope, locked versions, complete evidence, VEP, RRP, applicable EDP/DGS, DQS, IPS, monitoring, EDRP, owners, limits, kill switch, rollback, incident response and audit. No silent exception, version change, scope expansion, disabled control or monitoring gap. Readiness is neither proof of edge nor deployment authority by itself.

## 3. Production Readiness Identity Model

Each review receives immutable Production Readiness ID, record version, strategy/portfolio/deployment-candidate identities, target stage, environment, scope, version-lock set, owner, and assessment time. Any material scope, version, environment, authority, control or target-stage change creates a new review version.

## 4. Production Readiness Object Model

The record shall define owner/responsible agent; strategy, portfolio, candidate, stage, scope, environment, market, regime, capital and risk identities; DQS, IPS, EEP, VEP, RRP, EDP, DGS, MEDS, EDRP, PCS and all research/robustness records; evidence, Validation, risk, authority, governance, data, parity, monitoring, ownership, controls, kill, rollback, incident, alert, logging, audit, configuration, locks, environment, dependency, security, liquidity, capacity and cost status; gaps, limitations, contradictions, exceptions/expiry, follow-up, classification, confidence, maturity, institutional links, timestamps, and audit trail.

## 5. Production Readiness Lifecycle

**Proposed → Readiness Scope Registered → Evidence Intake Completed → Version Locks Reviewed → Validation Readiness Reviewed → Risk Readiness Reviewed → Executive Authority Reviewed → Deployment Governance Reviewed → Data and Implementation Reviewed → Monitoring and Response Reviewed → Operational Controls Reviewed → Readiness Gaps Registered → Readiness Classified → Evidence Packaged → Returned → Rejected → Challenged → Superseded → Archived**

Every transition records exact versions, prior state, authority, owner, evidence, rationale, conditions, gaps, outputs and time. No readiness domain may be skipped silently.

## 6. Readiness Scope Rules

Scope declares strategy/portfolio versions, target stage, markets, instruments, venues, timeframe, regimes, capital, exposure, leverage, environment, implementation, data, monitoring, operational hours, authority period, consumers and prohibited uses. Classification applies only to that scope.

## 7. Evidence Completeness Rules

The review verifies strategy identity/version, architecture, implementation, DQS, IPS, XDS, EEP, VEP, RRP, applicable EDP/DGS/MEDS/PCS, WFRS, MCSTS, PSS, ODS, contradictions, limitations, unresolved gaps and prohibited interpretations. Missing mandatory evidence is a block, not a blank checkbox.

## 8. Strategy Lifecycle Readiness Rules

SLS state must be consistent with the requested target stage and all preceding transitions. Degraded, suspended, retired, expired, superseded or returned strategy versions cannot be declared ready outside renewed governance.

## 9. Experiment Evidence Readiness Rules

EEP identity/version, frozen status, exact implementation/data/strategy versions, design conformance, robustness, negative controls, deviations, limitations and reproducibility must support the intended scope. Experiment success alone provides no operational readiness.

## 10. Validation Readiness Rules

Issued VEP must cover exact versions and scope, preserve accepted/rejected/qualified evidence, conditions, limitations, expiry and revalidation triggers, and contain no unresolved block relevant to the target stage. Validation is necessary but insufficient.

## 11. Risk Review Readiness Rules

Issued RRP must cover exact scope and define capital, exposure, leverage, loss/drawdown, market/regime, liquidity, execution, portfolio, monitoring, kill, suspension, reactivation and expiry conditions. Required mitigation must be verified, not merely promised.

## 12. Executive Authority Readiness Rules

Issued EDP must authorize the exact target stage, versions, scope, capital, exposure, conditions, monitoring, kill, expiry and reassessment triggers. Deployment candidate, paper, limited-live and live-active authority remain distinct; readiness cannot broaden the verdict.

## 13. Deployment Governance Readiness Rules

DGS record must be issued, current and scope-matched with strategy/implementation/configuration/environment/data locks, operational ownership, controls, monitoring, rollback and kill readiness. PRC informs DGS; it does not replace activation authority.

## 14. Data Quality Readiness Rules

DQS records must authorize the exact research/live purpose, source, symbol, fields, timeframe, timezone/calendar, preprocessing, freshness, revisions and fallback. Limitations, quarantine, cross-source conflicts and live/backtest mismatch are reflected in controls and monitoring.

## 15. Implementation Parity Readiness Rules

IPS must bind exact architecture, research, paper/live, risk-control and monitoring versions and classify all mismatches. Critical, blocking, invalidating or material unknown differences prevent applicable readiness; limitations travel into DGS/MEDS.

## 16. Portfolio Readiness Rules

Where portfolio context applies, PCS must cover exact constituents, versions, allocations, risk budgets, exposures, constraints, dependence, liquidity, capacity, stress and monitoring. Component readiness cannot excuse an unready portfolio interaction.

## 17. Monitoring Readiness Rules

MEDS record defines monitored identity, baseline/version, health and edge states, metrics, warning/breach/kill thresholds, cadence, data source, owners, alerts, escalation, false-alarm handling, evidence packaging, coverage and audit. Monitoring must be active before production-like operation.

## 18. Edge Decay Response Readiness Rules

EDRP path identifies trigger intake, evidence freezing, severity, cause separation, authority, watch/degradation/breach actions, suspension, rollback, all return paths, revalidation, reactivation and retirement. Owners, deadlines and communication routes are assigned before activation.

## 19. Operational Ownership Rules

Primary, backup, escalation, risk, incident, rollback, monitoring, communication and executive owners are registered, active, authorized, available for the operational window, and aware of duties. Shared responsibility without named accountability is not readiness.

## 20. Control Readiness Rules

Capital, position, exposure, leverage, loss, drawdown, liquidity, capacity, venue, regime, trading-pause, suspension, reactivation and manual-override controls are versioned, scope-matched, tested, observable and owned. Failed or untested critical controls block readiness.

## 21. Kill Switch Readiness Rules

Kill triggers, scope, authority, execution path, open-order/position handling, safe state, acknowledgement, failure mode, manual fallback, communications, evidence and restoration prohibition are tested. A kill switch that exists only on paper is a decorative button.

## 22. Rollback Readiness Rules

Rollback defines trigger, exact safe target version/state, authority, owner, position/capital treatment, configuration/data/environment restoration, verification, monitoring, communication and audit. The target must itself be authorized and reconstructable.

## 23. Incident Response Readiness Rules

Incident taxonomy, severity, detection, containment, owners, escalation, evidence freeze, communications, operational continuity, suspension, recovery, review, learning and closure are defined for data, execution, control, platform, security, governance and market events.

## 24. Communication Readiness Rules

Notification channels, recipients, acknowledgement, severity routing, deadlines, redundancy, message content, escalation and outage fallback are verified. Critical communications cite exact identity, scope, authority, action and evidence; delivery failure is itself an incident.

## 25. Configuration Readiness Rules

Every operational setting has identity, version, owner, authorized value/range, source decision, environment, dependency, change control, drift detection, restore point and audit reference. Defaults and implicit platform settings are configuration and must be governed.

## 26. Version Lock Readiness Rules

Locks cover strategy, implementation, configuration, parameter, dataset, feature, regime, monitoring, risk control, deployment, portfolio, rollback target, environment and dependencies. Unknown, floating, mismatched or mutable critical versions block production-like operation.

## 27. Environment Readiness Rules

Environment identity, platform, time, locale, precision, resources, connectivity, persistence, restart/state recovery, limits, dependencies, maintenance, fallback and parity with reviewed behavior are documented and tested for target scope.

## 28. Data Pipeline Readiness Rules

Source identity, acquisition, timeliness, completeness, mapping, timezone, calendar, preprocessing, validation checks, stale/missing/duplicate behavior, revision handling, fallback, quarantine, alerts and recovery comply with DQS and MEDS.

## 29. Execution Readiness Rules

Venue/account/symbol, order types, routing, acknowledgement, fill/partial/reject/cancel handling, latency, tick/lot/minimums, margin, leverage, outages, restart, state reconciliation and IPS evidence match authorized assumptions and risk controls.

## 30. Cost and Slippage Readiness Rules

Fees, spread, slippage, funding, borrow, rollover, impact, currency conversion, effective dates, tiers, stress bounds and monitoring are consistent with EEP/VEP/RRP/DGS. Material deterioration has predefined EDRP action.

## 31. Liquidity and Capacity Readiness Rules

Instrument/venue liquidity, participation, exit and simultaneous-exit capacity, stressed depth, crowding, capital ceiling, spread/impact sensitivity, portfolio competition and monitoring support the exact target allocation. Scaling beyond evidence is not ready.

## 32. Risk Limit Readiness Rules

All RRP/EDP/DGS capital, position, exposure, leverage, market, regime, loss, drawdown, liquidity, capacity and portfolio limits are translated consistently into observable, enforceable, tested controls with precedence, breach and escalation behavior.

## 33. Alerting Readiness Rules

Alerts cover data, IPS, signals, execution, positions, risks, limits, monitoring, control, kill, environment, dependency and communication health. Thresholds, severity, deduplication, routing, acknowledgement, escalation, test evidence and outage fallback are current.

## 34. Logging and Audit Readiness Rules

Governed records capture input/version identity, calculations, signals, state, orders, fills, costs, positions, controls, alerts, actions, owners, timestamps, overrides and failures with integrity, retention, access and reconstruction sufficient for EES/ART.

## 35. Reproducibility Readiness Rules

Authorized reviewers can reproduce reference inputs, configurations, state, key decisions, expected behavior, readiness checks and evidence from registered artifacts and versions. Live nondeterminism is bounded and observable; irreproducible critical behavior blocks readiness.

## 36. Security and Access Readiness Rules

Access is least-authority, registered, role-bound, reviewable and revocable. Critical actions, configuration, controls, overrides, deployment, monitoring and evidence have ownership and separation appropriate to their authority. Access failure or ambiguity is contained and escalated.

## 37. Dependency Readiness Rules

Data vendors, venues, platforms, calendars, libraries, services, models, configurations, credentials, clocks, communications and human/agent owners have identities, versions where applicable, health criteria, failure effects, monitoring, fallback, replacement authority and expiry.

## 38. Failure Mode Readiness Rules

Known strategy, data, feature, regime, implementation, execution, liquidity, capacity, portfolio, control, monitoring, dependency, operational, security and governance failures have detection, severity, containment, owner, response path, communication, recovery and learning requirements.

## 39. Readiness Gap Rules

Every gap has domain, evidence, severity, scope, owner, deadline, blocking status, interim control, remediation, verification and re-entry condition. Critical gaps block classification; noncritical gaps appear as exact limitations with expiry and monitoring.

## 40. Readiness Exception Rules

Exceptions identify rule, rationale, evidence, scope, risk, compensating controls, authority, owner, start, expiry, monitoring and revocation. They cannot waive identity, mandatory authority, critical controls, kill, rollback, monitoring, evidence custody or reconstruction, and never silently become precedent.

## 41. Readiness Classification Model

Permitted classifications are **PRODUCTION READY**, **PRODUCTION READY WITH LIMITATIONS**, **PAPER READY**, **LIMITED LIVE READY**, **READINESS DEGRADED**, **READINESS INCONCLUSIVE**, **READINESS FAILED**, and **READINESS INVALID**. Each states exact target stage, versions, scope, gaps, exceptions, conditions, expiry and prohibited interpretations.

## 42. Readiness Confidence Model

Confidence is **HIGH**, **MEDIUM**, **LOW**, **INSUFFICIENT**, or **CONFLICTED**, based on evidence completeness, test coverage, version certainty, owner/control availability, reproducibility, auditability and contradictions—not expected profitability.

## 43. Readiness Maturity Model

Maturity is **DESIGNED**, **EVIDENCE_READY**, **PAPER_CANDIDATE**, **LIMITED_LIVE_CANDIDATE**, **LIVE_ACTIVE_CANDIDATE**, **MONITORING_READY**, **RETURNED**, **REJECTED**, or **SUPERSEDED**. It describes review progress and cannot grant stage authority.

## 44. Deviation and Amendment Rules

Deviation records original requirement, event, timing, owner, authority, affected versions/scope, evidence and control impact, temporary containment, expiry and remediation. Amendments create prospective record versions and trigger impact review; frozen readiness evidence is never edited in place.

## 45. Production Readiness Evidence Packaging

The frozen package contains readiness identity/version; exact strategy/portfolio/candidate/stage/scope and all locks; all linked evidence and authority; status for every mandatory domain; owners and controls; kill, rollback, incident, alerts, logs, security, dependencies and failure modes; tests, gaps, exceptions, expiry, classification, confidence, maturity, limitations, contradictions, prohibited interpretations and follow-up.

## 46. Production Readiness Audit and Reconstruction

An independent reviewer must reconstruct scope, evidence intake, exact versions, domain checks, tests, gaps, exceptions, owners, authorities, classification, handoffs, changes and resulting operation without chat history or private memory. Audit preserves custody, artifacts, authorship, timestamps, transitions, challenges and supersession.

## 47. Integration with MACP

MACP carries version-bound readiness request, gap, block, exception, classification, challenge, handoff, degradation and revocation messages. Exact scope, target stage, evidence, versions, authority, owner, conditions and expiry are mandatory; messages do not replace records.

## 48. Integration with SMI

SMI stores current readiness state, scope, locks, domain statuses, gaps, exceptions, owners, authority, conditions, expiry and evidence references. Conflicting, missing or stale state blocks WOE progression.

## 49. Integration with EES

EES governs provenance, admissibility, uncertainty, contradiction, freezing, chain of custody, challenge and consumer traceability. Gaps, failures, exceptions and limitations travel with the readiness claim.

## 50. Integration with WOE

WOE enforces intake, domain dependencies, owners, stage gates, gap remediation, returns, rejection, challenge, escalation, expiry and archive. No stage promotion occurs from PRC alone, and no missing mandatory gate may be skipped.

## 51. Integration with Agent Registry

Only registered agents with explicit evidence, Validation, risk, decision, deployment, monitoring, incident, security, message, memory and workflow rights may attest their domains. No single agent may self-certify authority it does not possess.

## 52. Integration with Artifact Registry

ART registers checklists, evidence references, locks, configurations, tests, gaps, exceptions, controls, incidents, decisions and packages with lineage, versions, owners, consumers, freeze, retention and supersession.

## 53. Integration with Strategy Lifecycle Standard

SLS provides exact state and allowable next transitions. PRC may support but cannot execute paper, limited-live, live-active, scaling, reactivation or retirement transitions without required EDP/DGS authority.

## 54. Integration with Autonomous Research Pipeline

ARP supplies reconstructable research and evidence lineage and receives exact return stage, missing artifact, owner and re-entry condition. Production gaps cannot be patched by unstored pipeline assumptions.

## 55. Integration with Data Quality Standard

DQS supplies purpose-specific eligibility, lineage, versions, temporal integrity, defects, revisions, live checks, quarantine and limitations. Production data substitution or revision triggers readiness impact review.

## 56. Integration with Feature Factory Standard

FFS supplies exact feature versions, inputs, transformations, scope, stability, drift and monitoring obligations. Missing feature observability or unreviewed replacement blocks feature-dependent readiness.

## 57. Integration with Regime Classification Standard

RCS supplies authorized regime scope, taxonomy, confidence, uncertainty, transition and drift rules. Readiness states how operation behaves in authorized, transition, uncertain and excluded regimes.

## 58. Integration with Edge Discovery Standard

EDS supplies mechanism, conditions, failure modes, controls and prohibited interpretations. PRC verifies operational observability of those conditions but cannot validate the edge.

## 59. Integration with Strategy Family Selection Standard

SFSS supplies family requirements, execution/cost/risk implications, failures and monitoring needs. Readiness checks that architecture and controls preserve the selected family scope.

## 60. Integration with Experiment Design Standard

XDS supplies preregistered implementation, data, assumptions, metrics, controls, criteria and deviations. Operational settings outside tested design are gaps or new scope, not harmless configuration.

## 61. Integration with Walk-Forward and Robustness Standard

WFRS supplies temporal robustness, failures, degradation and scope limits. PRC verifies relevant revalidation triggers and monitoring but cannot convert robustness into live authority.

## 62. Integration with Monte Carlo and Stress Testing Standard

MCSTS supplies path, cost, execution, liquidity, tail, regime and portfolio stress evidence and thresholds. PRC maps applicable failure scenarios to controls, kill, rollback, incident and monitoring paths.

## 63. Integration with Parameter Stability Standard

PSS supplies selected sets, islands, cliffs, interactions, limits and drift triggers. Exact parameters are locked; readiness prohibits silent retuning and defines response when stability assumptions fail.

## 64. Integration with Overfitting Defense Standard

ODS supplies research exposure, holdout, controls, complexity and residual overfitting risk. PRC preserves limitations and prevents operational tuning from bypassing new ODS/Validation requirements.

## 65. Integration with Implementation Parity Standard

IPS supplies reference-to-research/paper/live/monitoring equivalence, tolerances and mismatches. PRC verifies exact target implementation and treats material unknown or control-related divergence as blocking.

## 66. Integration with Experiment Evidence Package

EEP supplies frozen, version-matched experimental evidence, controls, failures, deviations, assumptions and reproducibility. PRC never substitutes newer or partial results without a governed package.

## 67. Integration with Validation Evidence Package

VEP supplies issued verdict, scope, limitations, conditions, expiry and open questions. PRC checks compliance; it cannot broaden or rewrite Validation evidence.

## 68. Integration with Risk Review Package

RRP supplies issued verdict, limits, controls, monitoring, kill, suspension, reactivation and expiry. PRC verifies readiness of those obligations but cannot accept risk.

## 69. Integration with Executive Decision Package

EDP supplies exact stage authority, scope, capital/exposure, conditions, monitoring, kill and expiry. PRC classification cannot exceed, replace or imply Executive Decision.

## 70. Integration with Deployment Governance Standard

DGS consumes PRC evidence when determining readiness and locks exact authorized versions, controls and owners before activation. DGS alone governs operational activation, downgrade, rollback, suspension and deployment retirement.

## 71. Integration with Monitoring and Edge Decay Standard

MEDS supplies baseline, thresholds, health models, cadence, ownership, alerting, evidence and drift monitoring. PRC verifies activation readiness and continued coverage for the exact stage.

## 72. Integration with Edge Decay Response Playbook

EDRP supplies operational paths for warning, degradation, breach, cause separation, limits, suspension, rollback, return, revalidation, reactivation and retirement. PRC checks owners, authority, communications and executable response readiness.

## 73. Integration with Portfolio Construction Standard

PCS supplies exact constituents, allocations, budgets, exposures, dependencies, constraints, liquidity/capacity, stress and monitoring. Portfolio production readiness cannot be inferred from component readiness alone.

## 74. Governance

Deployment governance owns the checklist and operational-readiness determination; domain owners attest only their governed evidence; Validation, Risk and Executive authorities remain independent. Changes require versioning, impact analysis, prospective approval and migration. Exceptions are scoped, owned, time-bounded and auditable and cannot waive identity, exact authority, critical controls, kill, rollback, monitoring, custody or reconstruction.

Mandatory domains are evidence, Validation, risk, Executive authority, DGS, DQS, IPS, PCS, MEDS, EDRP, ownership, controls, kill, rollback, incident, communication, configuration, locks, environment, data pipeline, execution, liquidity/capacity, costs, alerts, logs, audit, security, dependencies and failure modes. Mandatory risks include missing or invalid evidence/authority, drift, failures, contradictions and silent exceptions across those domains.

Every future AI Quant Lab paper, limited-live, live-active, scaled, monitoring or production-like readiness claim must conform to PRC before institutional reliance. Readiness failure, exception, rejection, invalidation, supersession, suspension or retirement remains historically reconstructable and learnable.
