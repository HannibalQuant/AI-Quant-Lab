# AI QUANT LAB — Portfolio Construction Standard v1.0

**Document Class:** Constitutional Standard  
**Authority:** AI Quant Lab Portfolio, Evidence, and Risk Governance  
**Status:** v1.0  
**Applies To:** Every institutional multi-strategy combination, allocation, diversification claim, portfolio candidate, deployment scope, and monitoring baseline

## 1. Portfolio Construction Philosophy

A portfolio is a governed system of interacting risks, not a collection of individually attractive strategies. Construction must explain why each component belongs, what shared dependencies it creates, how exposures combine in ordinary and adverse conditions, and where capital, liquidity, execution, and authority impose boundaries. Apparent diversification has no standing without evidence of behavior during losses, tails, regime shifts, and simultaneous exits.

## 2. Portfolio Construction Principles

No portfolio without identity, validated component scope, risk context, inclusion review, allocation and risk budgets, exposure maps, correlation, drawdown and tail review, regime analysis, liquidity/capacity, execution/cost interactions, constraints, stress, and monitoring. No silent constituent, weight, constraint, leverage, or scope change. Construction is neither Validation, risk approval, Executive Decision, nor deployment authority.

## 3. Portfolio Identity Model

Every portfolio and candidate receives immutable Portfolio ID, record version, purpose, owner, composition identity, constituent strategy versions, allocation version, constraint version, and authorized scope. Material composition, weight, risk budget, constraint, or dependency change creates a new version; rejected and superseded identities remain reconstructable.

## 4. Portfolio Construction Object Model

The record shall define portfolio identity/name/purpose/scope/lifecycle/candidate status; owner and responsible agent; included/excluded strategy identities, versions, states and rationale; linked VEP/RRP/EDP/DGS/MEDS, market/regime/feature/edge/family/XDS/WFRS/MCSTS/PSS/ODS/IPS/DQS records; universe, assets, venues, instruments, symbols, timeframes, families and regimes; allocation and risk budgets; all maximum/minimum weights, exposures, leverage and loss limits; correlation, drawdown, tail, exposure and overlap maps; liquidity, capacity, execution, cost and stress evidence; classification, confidence, maturity, limitations, contradictions, deviations, follow-up, institutional links, timestamps, and audit trail.

## 5. Portfolio Construction Lifecycle

**Proposed → Portfolio Scope Registered → Candidate Strategies Received → Inclusion Eligibility Reviewed → Exposure Map Completed → Correlation Review Completed → Drawdown Interaction Reviewed → Liquidity and Capacity Reviewed → Constraint Plan Registered → Portfolio Stress Reviewed → Portfolio Classified → Evidence Packaged → Returned → Rejected → Challenged → Superseded → Archived**

Each transition records authority, owner, exact input versions, evidence, rationale, conditions, outputs, and time. Return and challenge preserve the earlier state and decision path.

## 6. Portfolio Scope Rules

Scope declares institutional purpose, eligible strategies and versions, markets, instruments, venues, asset classes, timeframes, regimes, capital range, risk budget, leverage, execution context, horizon, constraints, intended consumers, and prohibited uses. Unreviewed expansion has no portfolio authority.

## 7. Strategy Inclusion Eligibility Rules

Each included strategy shall have governed identity and architecture; eligible DQS data; IPS implementation; EEP evidence; VEP scope; RRP context where risk acceptability is claimed; applicable EDP/DGS/MEDS status; precise market, regime, timeframe, parameter, capital and risk limits; limitations and contradictions. Inclusion is purpose- and version-specific.

## 8. Strategy Exclusion Rules

Excluded candidates, versions, reasons, evidence, failed requirements, conflicts, reconsideration conditions, and dates remain visible. Exclusion cannot hide adverse candidates, manufacture diversification, or reset selection history under ODS.

## 9. Capital Allocation Rules

Allocation defines eligible capital, reserve, weights, bounds, unit, valuation basis, rebalance behavior, cash treatment, leverage, precision, rationale, dependencies, and loss absorption. Allocation follows scope and evidence; it cannot broaden a component's authority or be justified by headline return alone.

## 10. Risk Budget Rules

Risk budget identifies total and component budgets for volatility, loss, drawdown, tails, exposure, leverage, liquidity, execution, concentration, and operational failure. Allocation and risk contribution are distinguished. Breach ownership, escalation, suspension, and reset rules are explicit.

## 11. Exposure Mapping Rules

Maps show strategy, market, asset class, venue, instrument, symbol, timeframe, regime, family, feature, signal, factor, liquidity, execution, cost, gross, net, leverage, conditional, hidden, and cumulative exposures under current and stressed conditions. Offset in one dimension cannot conceal concentration in another.

## 12. Correlation Review Rules

Review includes return, signal, trade, position, exposure and P&L correlation; rolling, conditional, nonlinear, regime, stress and crisis dependence; sampling uncertainty, instability, data basis, and contradictions. Static full-period correlation is descriptive, not sufficient evidence of diversification.

## 13. Drawdown Correlation Rules

Analysis identifies concurrent drawdown, start and recovery alignment, depth and duration dependence, loss clustering, underwater overlap, worst-period contribution, and conditional correlation when portfolio capital is impaired. Favorable average correlation cannot average away shared drawdowns.

## 14. Tail Dependence Rules

Review addresses joint extreme losses, co-exceedance, downside asymmetry, gaps, liquidity tails, execution tails, nonlinear contagion, crisis regimes, confidence and sample limitations. Missing tail evidence constrains risk-reduction claims.

## 15. Regime Exposure Rules

RCS labels, confidence, transitions and uncertainty map each strategy's expected activity, failure, correlation, liquidity and risk contribution. Portfolio regime concentration includes apparently different strategies dependent on the same environmental condition.

## 16. Market Exposure Rules

Direct and indirect exposure by market, instrument, venue, asset class, currency, geography and benchmark is aggregated across positions, features, funding, collateral and hedges. Symbol diversity does not establish market diversity.

## 17. Timeframe Exposure Rules

Signal horizons, holding periods, decision cadence, common bar boundaries, overnight/weekend exposure, liquidity windows and simultaneous reaction are mapped. Different chart timeframes can retain the same underlying risk horizon.

## 18. Strategy Family Exposure Rules

Family concentration is assessed by mechanism, not label. Trend, breakout, momentum, pullback, mean reversion, volatility, liquidity, session, cross-market, adaptive and hybrid components expose shared success and failure dependencies.

## 19. Feature and Signal Overlap Rules

FFS features, inputs, transformations, thresholds, regimes, signal dates, directions, confidence and suppression logic are compared. Redundant measures or synchronized signals create concentration even when implementation details differ.

## 20. Factor Exposure Rules

Factor maps identify direction, beta, volatility, liquidity, momentum, carry, value, size, duration, currency, commodity, equity, crypto, funding, convexity and other material common drivers. Factor identity, method, stability, residual risk and uncertainty are recorded.

## 21. Liquidity and Capacity Rules

Review covers venue/instrument liquidity, order size, participation, spread, slippage, impact, exit capacity, simultaneous liquidation, stressed depth, crowding, outage, capacity decay, capital ceiling, and monitoring. Individual capacity cannot simply be summed when constituents compete for the same liquidity.

## 22. Execution Interaction Rules

Order timing, direction, netting, crossing, queue, concurrency, venue limits, throttling, margin, collateral, partials, rejects, cancels, latency, routing, and kill-switch interactions are assessed. One strategy's execution may alter another's fill or risk state.

## 23. Cost Interaction Rules

Portfolio cost evidence includes commissions, fees, spreads, impact, funding, borrow, rollover, currency conversion, turnover, netting benefits, lost netting, minimum charges and stressed cost. Cost assumptions bind DQS/IPS evidence and scale; discounts are not presumed.

## 24. Position Overlap Rules

Same, related, offsetting, derivative, synthetic and collateral-linked positions are aggregated by economic exposure. Gross and net views coexist. Hedging claims disclose basis, timing, sizing, liquidity and failure risk.

## 25. Concentration Risk Rules

Concentration limits cover strategy, market, asset, venue, instrument, timeframe, family, regime, feature, signal, factor, liquidity, execution, cost, collateral and operational dependencies. Hidden leverage, conditional concentration and crisis convergence are mandatory review items.

## 26. Diversification Claim Rules

A diversification claim requires stable and conditional evidence across returns, signals, positions, drawdowns, tails, regimes, crises, liquidity and execution. It states scope, horizon, uncertainty, failure conditions and expiry. Low average correlation, strategy count, or different names cannot establish diversification.

## 27. Portfolio Constraint Rules

Constraints define minimum/maximum weights, gross/net exposure, leverage, turnover, loss/drawdown, concentration, liquidity, capacity, venue, market, regime, factor, execution and operational bounds; precedence, conflict resolution, owner, monitoring, breach and release. Constraints are enforceable and versioned.

## 28. Portfolio Rebalancing Rules

Rebalancing declares triggers, cadence, information set, target method, tolerance bands, turnover and costs, unavailable strategies, partial execution, drift, failure, risk events, and authority. Rebalancing cannot silently become optimization or scope expansion.

## 29. Portfolio Scaling Rules

Scaling requires evidence that capital, liquidity, capacity, impact, concentration, margin, correlation, drawdown, tail, operational and monitoring behavior remain inside approved bounds. Scaling is a new governed decision, never an automatic reward for recent performance.

## 30. Portfolio Stress Testing Rules

Stress covers simultaneous strategy failure, correlated drawdown, correlation convergence, regime shift, volatility expansion, liquidity contraction, spread/slippage, delay, missed trades, outage, halt, scaling, tail events, funding shock, leverage constraint, forced deleveraging, monitoring failure and kill-switch interaction. Scenarios preserve MCSTS assumptions, thresholds, limitations and custody.

## 31. Portfolio Failure Mode Rules

The record identifies common mechanism failure, regime misclassification, shared data/feature/implementation dependence, model crowding, liquidity run, execution cascade, margin spiral, collateral impairment, hedge failure, control conflict, monitoring blind spot, governance breach and simultaneous suspension. Owners, detection, containment and learning are explicit.

## 32. Portfolio Monitoring Rules

MEDS obligations cover component identity/state, weights, allocations, budgets, all exposures, correlations, drawdown interaction, tail indicators, liquidity, capacity, costs, fills, constraints, breaches, data, IPS drift, controls, alerts and portfolio health. Coverage precedes deployment candidacy.

## 33. Portfolio Degradation Rules

Degradation occurs when evidence, correlation, diversification, liquidity, capacity, execution, cost, component health, monitoring, limits, or operational reliability weakens. The record narrows authority, sets enhanced monitoring, remediation, owner, deadline, reassessment and escalation.

## 34. Portfolio Suspension Rules

Critical exposure, loss, leverage, liquidity, correlation, data, implementation, monitoring, control, governance or simultaneous-failure breach triggers governed suspension. Suspension defines affected scope, safe state, authority, incident evidence, communication and required review; time passing cannot self-release it.

## 35. Portfolio Reactivation Rules

Reactivation requires root cause, remediated exact versions, updated evidence, retested constraints/controls, renewed component eligibility, RRP/EDP/DGS authority where applicable, monitoring readiness, conditions and expiry. Partial reactivation is separately scoped.

## 36. Portfolio Retirement Rules

Retirement records decision authority, reason, composition, final exposures, closure, unresolved liabilities, evidence, incidents, failures, learning, consumer notice, retention and reconstruction. Retired components and portfolios cannot silently re-enter under old authority.

## 37. Portfolio Classification Model

Permitted classifications are **PORTFOLIO ELIGIBLE**, **PORTFOLIO ELIGIBLE WITH LIMITATIONS**, **PORTFOLIO FRAGILE**, **PORTFOLIO INCONCLUSIVE**, **PORTFOLIO REJECTED**, and **PORTFOLIO INVALID**. Classification states versions, scope, constraints, residual risks, limitations, consumers, expiry and prohibited interpretations.

## 38. Portfolio Confidence Model

Confidence is **HIGH**, **MEDIUM**, **LOW**, **INSUFFICIENT**, or **CONFLICTED**, based on component evidence, coverage, stability, stress, dependency observability, liquidity realism, custody, reproducibility and contradictions—not portfolio return.

## 39. Portfolio Maturity Model

Maturity is **DESIGNED**, **EVIDENCE_READY**, **VALIDATION_CANDIDATE**, **RISK_REVIEW_CANDIDATE**, **EXECUTIVE_DECISION_CANDIDATE**, **DEPLOYMENT_CANDIDATE**, **MONITORING_READY**, **RETURNED**, **REJECTED**, or **SUPERSEDED**. Maturity conveys process readiness, not approval.

## 40. Deviation and Amendment Rules

Deviations identify original rule, changed constituent/allocation/constraint/assumption, cause, timing, information available, authority, affected evidence and consumers, temporary controls, expiry and remediation. Amendments create prospective versions and trigger impact review; frozen records remain unchanged.

## 41. Portfolio Evidence Packaging

The frozen package includes portfolio identity/version/scope; included/excluded strategies, versions and rationale; all VEP/RRP/EDP/DGS/MEDS and DQS/IPS/research links; allocation and risk budgets; constraints and limits; exposure and overlap maps; correlation, drawdown and tail evidence; regime/market/timeframe/factor analysis; liquidity/capacity/execution/cost review; stress and failure modes; classification, confidence, maturity, limitations, contradictions, deviations, prohibited interpretations and follow-up.

## 42. Portfolio Audit and Reconstruction

An independent reviewer must reconstruct candidate population, eligibility, exclusions, versions, allocation, budgets, exposures, dependencies, constraints, tests, stress, classifications, changes, decisions and consumers without chat history or private memory. Audit preserves custody, artifacts, integrity references, authorship, timestamps, transitions, challenges and supersession.

## 43. Integration with MACP

MACP carries version-bound proposal, inclusion, exclusion, exposure, breach, challenge, return, classification, handoff, suspension, reactivation and retirement messages. Portfolio and component versions, scope, authority, evidence, conditions and consumers are mandatory; messages do not replace records.

## 44. Integration with SMI

SMI stores governed current composition, versions, weights, budgets, exposures, constraints, classifications, health, blocks, conditions and expiry. Conflicting memory state or stale component identity blocks WOE progression.

## 45. Integration with EES

EES governs provenance, admissibility, uncertainty, contradictions, chain of custody, freezing, challenge and consumer traceability. Adverse dependencies, exclusions, stress failures and limitations travel with every portfolio claim.

## 46. Integration with WOE

WOE enforces ownership, component dependencies, stage gates, inclusion, exposure completion, stress, returns, rejection, escalation, suspension and re-entry. Missing eligibility, unclassified exposure, unresolved risk block or expired evidence prevents advancement.

## 47. Integration with Agent Registry

Only registered agents with explicit portfolio, Validation, risk, decision, evidence, message, memory and workflow rights may propose, review, classify, approve or consume portfolio records. Technical allocation capability creates no institutional authority.

## 48. Integration with Artifact Registry

ART registers candidate sets, allocation records, maps, matrices, constraints, tests, stress, reports, decisions, incidents and packages with lineage, versions, owners, consumers, freeze, retention and supersession. Artifacts cannot self-authorize capital.

## 49. Integration with Strategy Lifecycle Standard

Every constituent retains its own SLS identity, version, state, scope and controls. Portfolio inclusion does not advance or broaden a strategy. Component degradation, suspension, amendment or retirement triggers portfolio impact review.

## 50. Integration with Autonomous Research Pipeline

ARP supplies governed candidate and evidence paths and preserves rejected alternatives. Portfolio feedback creates explicit research, Validation, risk, deployment or monitoring workflows; it cannot silently alter upstream evidence.

## 51. Integration with Data Quality Standard

DQS governs all price, return, correlation, liquidity, cost, factor and monitoring inputs. Cross-asset alignment, point-in-time universes, missingness, revisions and source mismatch constrain portfolio evidence.

## 52. Integration with Feature Factory Standard

FFS supplies feature identities, versions, lineage, redundancy, scope and drift. Portfolio construction maps shared features and input dependence rather than treating strategy-level packaging as independence.

## 53. Integration with Regime Classification Standard

RCS supplies taxonomy, scope, confidence, uncertainty and transitions. Portfolio regime exposure and conditional dependence preserve component limitations and explicitly include unknown, mixed and transition states.

## 54. Integration with Edge Discovery Standard

EDS mechanisms reveal shared economic causes and alternative explanations. Combining edge candidates does not validate them; mechanism overlap and contradiction inform concentration and simultaneous failure.

## 55. Integration with Strategy Family Selection Standard

SFSS provides family identity, rationale, conditions and failure modes. Family labels are challenged against actual mechanisms, features and signals when assessing diversification.

## 56. Integration with Experiment Design Standard

XDS preregisters portfolio question, scope, candidate population, allocation/risk assumptions, metrics, controls, stress, criteria and interpretation boundaries before portfolio outcomes are known.

## 57. Integration with Walk-Forward and Robustness Standard

WFRS supports time-aligned component and portfolio evidence across folds, regimes and windows. Non-overlapping histories, asynchronous tests and survivor-only aggregation cannot establish portfolio robustness.

## 58. Integration with Monte Carlo and Stress Testing Standard

MCSTS governs joint paths, dependence, correlation shifts, loss clusters, execution/liquidity/cost stress, seeds, scenarios and failure thresholds. Portfolio stress cannot replace component evidence or RRP.

## 59. Integration with Parameter Stability Standard

PSS identifies whether allocations and combined behavior depend on fragile component regions. Portfolio construction may not hide unstable parameters through averaging or selective component weighting.

## 60. Integration with Overfitting Defense Standard

ODS receives portfolio candidate, exclusion, weighting, metric, constraint and stress-selection histories. Choosing constituents or weights after observing combined results consumes degrees of freedom and requires penalty or fresh evidence.

## 61. Integration with Implementation Parity Standard

IPS binds exact research, portfolio-accounting, paper, live, risk-control and monitoring implementations. Differences in netting, sizing, state, fills, costs, constraints or kill switches limit or block reliance.

## 62. Integration with Experiment Evidence Package

EEP supplies exact component evidence and versions. Portfolio evidence references rather than rewrites EEP findings and preserves negative controls, failure cases, limitations and contradictions.

## 63. Integration with Validation Evidence Package

VEP independently assesses portfolio evidence admissibility, component scope, dependence, robustness, selection bias, contradictions and limitations. Portfolio construction cannot manufacture scientific validity absent from constituents.

## 64. Integration with Risk Review Package

RRP owns institutional acceptability of capital, exposure, leverage, drawdown, tail, liquidity, execution, operational and portfolio interaction risk. PCS supplies evidence and proposed constraints but cannot accept risk or broaden validated scope.

## 65. Integration with Executive Decision Package

EDP alone issues portfolio advancement, return, rejection, suspension, reactivation or retirement authority within Validation and risk scope. Exact composition, versions, allocation limits, constraints, monitoring, expiry and unresolved issues are cited.

## 66. Integration with Deployment Governance Standard

DGS locks authorized constituents, implementations, configurations, weights/bounds, capital, exposures, controls, monitoring and rollback. Silent addition, removal, reweighting, scaling or constraint change blocks or suspends deployment.

## 67. Integration with Monitoring and Edge Decay Standard

MEDS monitors component and portfolio health, composition, exposures, limits, correlations, drawdown/tail dependence, regime concentration, liquidity/capacity, costs, execution, data, IPS, controls and alerts. Material drift triggers warning, degradation, suspension, review or retirement.

## 68. Governance

Portfolio governance owns construction identity, inclusion discipline, exposure maps, constraints and change control; independent Validation governs evidence admissibility; Risk Governance accepts or rejects risk; Executive Decision owns final authority. Changes require versioning, impact analysis, prospective approval, migration and monitoring. Exceptions are scoped, owned, time-bounded and auditable and cannot waive component eligibility, exposure visibility, risk blocks, custody or reconstruction.

Mandatory risks include concentration by strategy, market, asset, venue, timeframe, family, regime, feature, signal, factor, liquidity, execution and cost; drawdown correlation, tail dependence, hidden leverage, capacity, crowding, correlation breakdown, regime shift, simultaneous failure and monitoring blind spots. Mandatory dimensions include exact component versions and Validation, risk, deployment and monitoring scopes.

Every future AI Quant Lab portfolio candidate, strategy combination, allocation, diversification, correlation, risk-budget, deployment or monitoring claim must conform to PCS before institutional reliance. Failure, rejection, invalidation, supersession, suspension or retirement never erases portfolio history or learning.
