# AI QUANT LAB — Institutional Operating Playbook v1.0

**Document Class:** Constitutional Institutional Playbook  
**Authority:** AI Quant Lab Institutional Operations Governance  
**Status:** v1.0  
**Applies To:** Every institutional operating cycle, workflow queue, agent assignment, evidence/artifact/memory change, review, decision, deployment, monitoring event, incident, and learning update

## 1. Institutional Operating Philosophy

AI Quant Lab operates as a chain of governed institutional states, not a stream of agent activity. Work becomes institutional only when command, workflow, authority, evidence, artifact, memory, review, decision and learning are explicit and reconstructable. Daily operation preserves separation of duties while moving eligible work through constitutional gates.

## 2. Institutional Operating Principles

No operation without CM, WOE, AR, MACP, EES, ART and SMI discipline. No strategy progress outside SLS/ARP; no experiment without XDS; no Validation without frozen evidence; no risk without issued VEP; no Executive Decision without VEP/RRP; no deployment without EDP/DGS/PRC/DQS/IPS/MEDS/controls; no decay response without EDRP; no portfolio operation without PCS. No silent skip, mutation, exception, override, reactivation or deployment action.

## 3. Operating Identity Model

Each institutional cycle receives immutable Operating Cycle ID, Operating Record ID/version, operating date/period, owner, classification, scope and opening time. Corrected cycles link but never overwrite originals. All commands, workflows, agents, artifacts, evidence, memory, strategies, portfolios, deployments, monitoring and incidents within scope reference the cycle.

## 4. Operating Object Model

The record shall define cycle identity/date/owner/responsible agent; all command/workflow/agent/artifact/evidence/memory/strategy/portfolio/deployment/monitoring/incident identities; open/active/blocked/returned/rejected/completed queues; intake/assignment and every domain activity summary; evidence/artifact/memory changes; emergency, suspension, reactivation and retirement; blockers, risks, contradictions, exceptions, incidents, escalations, reviews, decisions, follow-up, learning/knowledge/state updates, metrics, limitations, deviations, source MACP and related institutional records, timestamps, and audit trail.

## 5. Operating Lifecycle

**Operating Cycle Proposed → Command Intake Open → Workflow Queue Reviewed → Agent Availability Reviewed → Evidence Queue Reviewed → Artifact Queue Reviewed → Active Workflows Routed → Blocked Workflows Escalated → Review Gates Processed → Decisions Recorded → Monitoring Signals Reviewed → Incidents Reviewed → Learning Updates Registered → Institutional State Updated → Operating Cycle Closed → Challenged → Corrected → Archived**

Every transition records prior state, authority, owner, evidence, rationale, open obligations and time. Closure requires reconciled queues and explicit carry-forward; correction creates a new record version.

## 6. Daily Operating Rules

Daily review states date, owner, active/blocked/completed/returned/rejected workflows, open commands/evidence/artifacts/memory changes, monitoring signals, breaches, incidents, emergency actions, agent capacity, overdue items, contradictions, escalations, decisions, priorities and audit references. Absence of activity is recorded, not inferred.

## 7. Command Intake Operating Rules

Every institutional instruction enters through CM identity, authority, scope, intent, ambiguity, route, evidence, output and audit checks. Duplicate, conflicting, urgent, emergency and incomplete commands are classified before workflow opening. Informal discussion remains noninstitutional until registered.

## 8. Workflow Opening Rules

WOE opening requires command identity, workflow type/owner, eligible agents, exact scope, inputs, outputs, evidence/artifact/memory requirements, dependencies, gates, criteria, blockers, escalation and archive policy. Missing owner or authority prevents registration.

## 9. Workflow Routing Rules

Routing matches command intent to constitutional domain, current state, registered agents, dependencies and review gates. Re-routing records cause, prior/new owner, evidence custody, deadline and downstream impact. Convenience cannot bypass independence or stage discipline.

## 10. Agent Assignment Rules

Assignment verifies AR identity/status/contract, single responsibility, authority, workflow eligibility, MACP rights, SMI/EES/ART rights, capacity, dependencies and conflict of interest. Suspended, retired or ineligible agents are blocked; technical capability does not confer institutional role.

## 11. Agent Handoff Rules

Handoffs identify source/recipient agents, command/workflow, state, exact scope, evidence/artifact/memory versions, completed work, open questions, blockers, limitations, deadline, acceptance and return criteria. Responsibility transfers only after acknowledged acceptance.

## 12. Evidence Operating Rules

Evidence queues track package identity/state, provenance, custody, admissibility, completeness, freeze, contradictions, amendments, invalidations, challenges, consumers, staleness and follow-up. Missing or unfrozen evidence blocks applicable review; adverse evidence cannot be suppressed to clear queues.

## 13. Artifact Operating Rules

Artifact queues track identity/version/state, owner, lineage, dependencies, consumers, validation/risk/decision state, freeze, supersession, deprecation, retirement, broken links, unauthorized mutation and archive. Material change after use creates a version and consumer impact review.

## 14. Shared Memory Operating Rules

SMI changes require source authority, provenance, object/version, owner, scope, timestamp, consumers and consistency check. Conflicting memory is visible and blocks dependent work. Chat history, private model memory and unstored assumptions cannot carry institutional state.

## 15. Research Operating Rules

Research work follows ARP with question, market/knowledge context, evidence standard, falsifiability, uncertainty, outputs, return path and learning. Research may propose candidates but cannot self-validate, accept risk or initiate deployment.

## 16. Market Discovery Operating Rules

MDRS operations consume eligible DQS data, registered universe/criteria and produce traceable opportunity/rejection records. Ranking changes, deferred candidates, contradictions and handoffs are reviewed; high score does not advance a strategy by itself.

## 17. Regime Classification Operating Rules

RCS operations bind exact market/data/timeframe/period/taxonomy and preserve confidence, uncertainty, alternatives, transitions and challenged labels. Current, historical and unknown states remain distinct; retrospective relabeling is prohibited.

## 18. Feature Factory Operating Rules

FFS operations govern feature identity/version/purpose, DQS lineage, transformations, temporal alignment, leakage/bias/redundancy/stability, scope and consumers. Candidate, restricted, superseded and retired features remain visible in operational state.

## 19. Edge Discovery Operating Rules

EDS operations preserve observed behavior, mechanism rationale, nulls, alternatives, regime/feature context, effect/sample/stability, costs, controls, contradictions and maturity. Failed and deferred candidates enter learning rather than disappearing.

## 20. Strategy Family Selection Operating Rules

SFSS operations compare credible families using frozen edge/market/regime/feature context and mechanism, execution, cost, risk and failure fit. Selection creates architecture handoff only; family changes require a new governed record.

## 21. Strategy Architecture Operating Rules

Architecture operations preserve hypothesis and mechanism, define modules/information/risk/failure/invalidation/experiments, and prohibit implementation leakage into scientific intent. Draft, approval, deviation and supersession states are explicit under SLS/ART.

## 22. Engineering Operating Rules

Engineering consumes approved architecture and produces exact versioned implementations/configurations with documented deviations, fidelity evidence and IPS reference behavior. It cannot change hypothesis, experiment results, VEP or RRP evidence.

## 23. Experiment Design Operating Rules

XDS operations preregister identity, purpose, scope, hypotheses/nulls, data partitions, variables/search, assumptions/costs, metrics, controls, criteria, deviations, reproducibility and output eligibility before interpretation.

## 24. Experiment Execution Operating Rules

Execution uses frozen XDS and exact versions, preserves all runs/failures/exclusions/interruptions/deviations, maintains custody and produces EEP inputs. Unauthorized redesign, selective reporting and post-result scope change trigger return or invalidation.

## 25. Robustness Operating Rules

WFRS and MCSTS operations preserve temporal partitions, folds, reoptimization, perturbations, seeds, scenarios, dependencies, failures, aggregation, classifications and limitations. Robustness evidence cannot validate, accept risk or authorize deployment.

## 26. Parameter Stability Operating Rules

PSS operations preserve search-space integrity, neighborhoods, islands, peaks, cliffs, interactions, boundary/failure zones and cross-context sensitivity. Post-result neighborhood redefinition and cherry-picked islands are blocked and recorded.

## 27. Overfitting Defense Operating Rules

ODS operations maintain full research/data/holdout/candidate/feature/parameter/market/timeframe/regime/metric history, negative controls, complexity, multiplicity, deflation, PBO, penalties and downgrades. Repeated access accumulates across cycles and agents.

## 28. Data Quality Operating Rules

DQS operations govern identity, lineage, versions, symbol/time, structure, market specifics, preprocessing, revisions, reconciliation, quarantine, remediation and eligibility. Data substitutions or repairs trigger downstream impact, not silent continuity.

## 29. Implementation Parity Operating Rules

IPS operations compare architecture/reference with research, Python, Pine, paper, live, risk-control and monitoring behavior at event level. All mismatches, tolerances, platform limits, retests and affected evidence remain visible.

## 30. Experiment Evidence Packaging Operating Rules

EEP assembly tracks mandatory sections, exact links, completeness, deviations, controls, failures, reproducibility, limitations, contradictory evidence, freeze and Validation readiness. Returned/amended packages create new versions and preserve the original.

## 31. Validation Operating Rules

Validation independently consumes frozen EEP/supporting evidence, defines scope, assesses admissibility/reproducibility/robustness/bias/leakage/overfitting/contradictions and issues VEP. It cannot accept risk, approve deployment or rewrite evidence.

## 32. Risk Review Operating Rules

Risk independently consumes issued VEP, preserves scope/limitations, evaluates market/execution/liquidity/model/data/tail/drawdown/portfolio/operational risk, defines limits/controls/monitoring/kill/suspension/reactivation and issues RRP. It cannot issue Executive or deployment authority.

## 33. Executive Decision Operating Rules

Executive operations consume issued VEP/RRP, preserve their boundaries, address open issues and issue EDP with exact scope, stage, conditions, capital/exposure, monitoring, kill, expiry and returns. Decision is not evidence and cannot silently ignore blocks.

## 34. Portfolio Construction Operating Rules

PCS operations govern component eligibility/versions, inclusion/exclusion, allocations, risk budgets, exposures, dependence, drawdown/tails, liquidity/capacity, constraints, stress and monitoring. Portfolio status never broadens component scope.

## 35. Production Readiness Operating Rules

PRC operations review exact target stage and versions across evidence, VEP/RRP/EDP/DGS, DQS/IPS/PCS/MEDS/EDRP, ownership, controls, kill, rollback, incident, logs, security, dependencies and gaps. Readiness classification does not activate operation.

## 36. Deployment Governance Operating Rules

DGS operations verify EDP authority, readiness, version locks, environment/data/execution/monitoring/controls/kill/ownership/rollback and issue governed candidate, paper, limited-live, live, downgrade, suspension, rollback or retirement records. No automatic promotion.

## 37. Monitoring Operating Rules

MEDS operations review monitored identities, baselines, health/edge states, warnings/degradation/breaches, false alarms, missing/stale signals, alerts, escalations, EDRP records, containment and required review. Monitoring silence is not evidence of health.

## 38. Edge Decay Response Operating Rules

EDRP operations freeze triggers, classify severity, separate causes, identify authority, apply watch/limits/containment/suspension/rollback/return, and govern revalidation/reactivation/retirement. No automatic retuning, reoptimization or reactivation.

## 39. Emergency Operating Rules

Emergency operations identify incident/trigger/severity/scope/authority/owner, execute only pre-authorized risk-reducing containment, freeze evidence, communicate, escalate and open follow-up review. Emergency action cannot create new scope or live authority.

## 40. Suspension Operating Rules

Suspension records authority, exact affected state/version/scope, trigger, safe state, open-position/capital treatment, controls, monitoring, investigation, communications and reactivation/retirement gates. It cannot self-expire.

## 41. Reactivation Operating Rules

Reactivation requires root cause, remediation, current DQS/IPS/regime evidence, applicable VEP/RRP/EDP/DGS/PRC, controls, monitoring, limited scope and expiry. It is a new governed decision, not restoration of old authority.

## 42. Retirement Operating Rules

Retirement records authority, reason, final strategy/portfolio/deployment state, obligations, unresolved risk, evidence, consumers, learning, archive and prohibited reuse. Renamed retired logic cannot bypass a new lifecycle.

## 43. Status Reporting Rules

Reports identify cycle, command/workflow/object versions, owner, state, completed/blocked work, missing evidence, risks, contradictions, incidents, next action, deadline, confidence, limitations and audit reference. Status never implies approval absent an authority record.

## 44. Review Cadence Rules

Mandatory cadences are daily operating review; command/workflow/evidence/artifact/agent-capacity/blocked-workflow/monitoring/risk-breach/deployment/portfolio/incident reviews; weekly governance; monthly institutional audit; quarterly architecture review; and post-incident, post-retirement and post-failure learning reviews. Each cadence has owner, scope, inputs, outputs, deadlines, carry-forward and audit.

## 45. Governance Cadence Rules

Weekly review addresses blocks, exceptions, authority conflicts, overdue gates and high-risk changes. Monthly audit tests records, custody, versions, controls, decisions and reconstruction. Quarterly review tests architecture coherence, standard conflicts, registry/contract fitness, systemic risk and required constitutional amendments.

## 46. Exception Handling Rules

Exceptions identify violated rule, rationale, evidence, scope, authority, owner, duration, risk, compensating controls, monitoring, consumers, revocation and review. They cannot waive identity, evidence custody, critical controls, independent gates, audit or reconstruction and never become silent precedent.

## 47. Escalation Rules

Escalation states issue, severity, affected scope, owner, failed route, evidence, containment, required authority, deadline and communication. Unresolved critical blockers, contradictions, breaches, ownership gaps or expired evidence are escalated; escalation does not authorize progression.

## 48. Audit and Reconstruction Rules

An independent reviewer must reconstruct every cycle's commands, workflows, assignments, evidence/artifact/memory changes, gates, decisions, deployments, monitoring, incidents, exceptions, learning and closure without chat history or private memory. Records preserve versions, custody, authorship, timestamps, transitions, challenges, corrections and archive.

## 49. Learning Loop Rules

Learning captures failed/returned/rejected workflows and strategies, invalidated evidence, failed Validation/risk/decisions, deployment/monitoring/portfolio incidents, decay, emergency actions, retirements, recurring blocks/contradictions and process/governance failures. Each update records evidence, cause, uncertainty, limitation, owner, proposed improvement and downstream impact.

## 50. Institutional Memory Update Rules

Knowledge and SMI updates follow accepted evidence and authorized state changes, preserve sources/versions/conflicts, link learning artifacts, identify consumers and never overwrite historical truth. Rejected and superseded conclusions remain discoverable with status.

## 51. Operating Metrics Rules

Metrics include command/workflow volumes and aging; active/blocked/returned/rejected/completed states; gate times; evidence/artifact defects; agent capacity/conflicts; overdue items; incidents; breaches; exceptions; rework; revalidation; monitoring coverage; response times; reconstruction failures; and learning closure. Metrics measure operating health, not strategy quality or approval.

## 52. Operating Failure Rules

Failure or downgrade includes unregistered command/workflow/agent action; unauthorized action; missing evidence custody/artifact identity/memory provenance; untracked deployment or breach; unresolved critical blocker/contradiction; silent exception/skip/mutation/replacement/deployment/reactivation; emergency action without record; or irreconstructable cycle. Classification is **OPERATING NORMAL**, **OPERATING WITH WATCH ITEMS**, **OPERATING DEGRADED**, **OPERATING BLOCKED**, **OPERATING INCIDENT**, **OPERATING SUSPENDED**, **OPERATING RECOVERY**, or **OPERATING INVALID**. Confidence is **HIGH**, **MEDIUM**, **LOW**, **INSUFFICIENT**, or **CONFLICTED**; maturity is **DESIGNED**, **ACTIVE**, **REVIEW_READY**, **DECISION_READY**, **MONITORING_READY**, **INCIDENT_ACTIVE**, **RECOVERY_ACTIVE**, **CLOSED**, **RETURNED**, **REJECTED**, **SUPERSEDED**, or **ARCHIVED**.

## 53. Integration with MACP

MACP transports attributable, version-bound commands, assignments, handoffs, status, evidence notices, reviews, decisions, incidents, escalations and closure. Messages preserve authority/scope/acknowledgement and link records; unregistered chat cannot change institutional state.

## 54. Integration with SMI

SMI holds current governed state for cycles, commands, workflows, agents, strategies, portfolios, deployments, monitoring, blockers and decisions. IOP reconciles changes and conflicts at each review and retains provenance.

## 55. Integration with EES

EES governs all evidence identity, provenance, admissibility, uncertainty, contradiction, freeze, custody, transfer, challenge, expiry and consumer traceability. Operational pressure cannot waive evidence requirements.

## 56. Integration with WOE

WOE is the mandatory state and routing authority for institutional work. IOP reviews every queue/state/dependency/gate/owner/deadline/return/escalation/cancellation/archive and prohibits off-workflow completion.

## 57. Integration with Agent Registry

AR governs agent identity, status, contract, authority, rights, workflow eligibility, delegation and suspension. IOP reviews capacity, assignments, conflicts, violations, handoffs and unauthorized actions every cycle.

## 58. Integration with Artifact Registry

ART governs every institutional product's identity, lineage, version, state, owner, consumers, freeze, supersession, retirement and archive. IOP reconciles artifact changes with commands, evidence, workflows and decisions.

## 59. Integration with Strategy Lifecycle Standard

SLS governs every strategy state and transition. IOP verifies exact identities, required packages/decisions, monitoring obligations and return paths and records every degraded, suspended, reactivated, retired and archived state.

## 60. Integration with Autonomous Research Pipeline

ARP governs research stage sequencing and handoffs. IOP reviews pipeline status, agents, artifacts, evidence, returns, failures and learning and prevents silent stage, scope, agent or evidence substitution.

## 61. Integration with Data Quality Standard

DQS governs datasets used across all operations. IOP reviews eligibility, quarantine, remediation, revisions, defects, live quality and consumer impact and blocks dependent work when purpose-specific eligibility fails.

## 62. Integration with Feature Factory Standard

FFS governs feature identity, lineage, scope, reviews, use and drift. IOP tracks feature candidates, approvals, restrictions, supersession and monitoring impacts across workflows.

## 63. Integration with Regime Classification Standard

RCS governs regime evidence, taxonomy, confidence, uncertainty, transitions and scope. IOP preserves current/challenged/unknown states and routes drift or mismatch through monitoring and EDRP.

## 64. Integration with Edge Discovery Standard

EDS governs pre-strategy candidates, mechanisms, controls and classifications. IOP records handoffs, rejected/deferred edges, contradictions and learning without converting discovery into Validation.

## 65. Integration with Strategy Family Selection Standard

SFSS governs family comparison, selection, limitations and architecture handoff. IOP blocks architecture work lacking a governed selection and records family changes as new workflows.

## 66. Integration with Experiment Design Standard

XDS governs preregistration and output eligibility. IOP verifies design state before execution, captures deviations and routes incomplete or invalid designs without result-driven amendments.

## 67. Integration with Walk-Forward and Robustness Standard

WFRS governs temporal robustness evidence. IOP tracks designs, folds, failures, classifications, returns and EEP/VEP handoff and prevents selected-fold progression.

## 68. Integration with Monte Carlo and Stress Testing Standard

MCSTS governs perturbations, seeds, scenarios, thresholds, failures and evidence. IOP preserves adverse results and ensures stress evidence is not mistaken for Validation or risk approval.

## 69. Integration with Parameter Stability Standard

PSS governs search spaces, neighborhoods, islands, cliffs, interactions and failure zones. IOP tracks exact parameter records and routes drift, fragility or amendment through required gates.

## 70. Integration with Overfitting Defense Standard

ODS governs cumulative research exposure, controls, complexity, multiplicity, deflation and penalties. IOP preserves command/workflow/search history across cycles so failed attempts cannot disappear.

## 71. Integration with Implementation Parity Standard

IPS governs equivalence across architecture, research, Python/Pine, paper/live, controls and monitoring. IOP blocks implementation-dependent evidence/deployment when versions or mismatch status are unresolved.

## 72. Integration with Experiment Evidence Package

EEP governs frozen experimental intake to Validation. IOP reviews assembly, completeness, returns, amendments, invalidations, custody and downstream consumers.

## 73. Integration with Validation Evidence Package

VEP governs independent scientific verdict. IOP protects Validation independence, tracks scope/limitations/conditions/expiry, routes returns and prevents operational reinterpretation.

## 74. Integration with Risk Review Package

RRP governs risk acceptability and limits. IOP tracks risk queues, mitigations, monitoring, kill/suspension/reactivation, expiry and breaches without broadening VEP scope.

## 75. Integration with Executive Decision Package

EDP governs final institutional lifecycle authority. IOP records pending/issued/returned/rejected/suspended/reactivated/retired decisions, conditions, expiry and affected workflows; status cannot replace an EDP.

## 76. Integration with Portfolio Construction Standard

PCS governs composition, allocations, budgets, exposures, dependencies, constraints, stress and monitoring. IOP reviews portfolio state, component changes, breaches and required portfolio-level responses.

## 77. Integration with Deployment Governance Standard

DGS governs readiness and operational activation/state. IOP reviews exact version locks, owners, controls, monitoring, incidents, rollback, suspension, reactivation and retirement; untracked changes are critical failures.

## 78. Integration with Monitoring and Edge Decay Standard

MEDS governs continuous health and evidence. IOP processes monitoring signals each cycle, identifies missing/stale coverage, records warnings/breaches and opens EDRP workflows where required.

## 79. Integration with Edge Decay Response Playbook

EDRP governs operational response, cause separation, authority, containment, returns, revalidation, reactivation and retirement. IOP tracks owners, deadlines, restrictions, communications and resolution without silent closure.

## 80. Integration with Production Readiness Checklist

PRC governs stage-specific readiness across every domain. IOP tracks gaps, exceptions, classification and expiry and prevents readiness from masquerading as DGS activation.

## 81. Integration with Command Manual

CM governs every instruction's identity, authority, scope, intent, route, constraints, output and audit. IOP opens no work without a valid command and reconciles command status with workflow and institutional state.

## 82. Governance

Institutional Operations governance owns this playbook, operating cycles, queues, cadences, status discipline and audit closure. Domain authorities retain substantive decisions; IOP coordinates but cannot override them. Amendments require versioning, impact analysis, approval and migration. Exceptions are scoped, owned, time-bounded and auditable and cannot waive constitutional authority, custody, critical controls or reconstruction.

Mandatory domains include command, workflow, agent, evidence, artifact, memory, research through monitoring, emergency, suspension/reactivation/retirement, audit and learning. Every operating handoff includes cycle identity/version/date/owner, workflow states, commands, evidence/artifact/memory/monitoring/incident states, blockers, contradictions, escalations, decisions, learning, limitations and audit reference.

Every future AI Quant Lab operating cycle, daily review, queue, assignment, evidence/artifact/memory review, monitoring/incident/emergency/governance review, learning update or operating-state claim must conform to IOP before institutional reliance. Closed, failed, corrected, rejected, suspended and archived cycles remain reconstructable and learnable.
